Here is the story of ringette:

Beginning:
Ringette was created in 1963 in Canada by Sam Jacks, a recreation director from North Bay, Ontario. He wanted to design a fast-paced winter team sport specifically for girls, since at the time girls weren't allowed to play 'boys' sports. The first official games were played in Ontario and Quebec, using ice skates, straight sticks, and a rubber ring. As the sport became more organized, official rules were developed, leagues were formed, and schools and community programs began offering ringette to young athletes across Canada.
Now:
Over the years, Ringette has grown into an internationally recognized sport with players of all ages and skill levels. Canada remains the leading country in ringette, but the sport has also spread to countries such as Finland, Sweden, and the United States. National and international competitions, including the World Ringette Championships, have helped increase its popularity and showcase the speed and teamwork involved in the game. Today, ringette continues to grow by encouraging inclusiveness, sportsmanship, and opportunities for female athletes in winter sports.
Future:
Since the start, ringette has seen a lot of success and growth. It continues to be a sport that promotes teamwork, strategy and resilience and will be the same in the future. In the future, hopefully ringette will be better known and played on a larger scale, with one main goal being its acceptance into the olympics.
